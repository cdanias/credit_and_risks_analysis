CLO liabilities vs arbitrage — the market pays managers to refinance, not to buy

Liability spreads sit at the year's tights while asset spreads compress: the arbitrage favours resets of old deals over new equity.

Restated fromE01E02E10
Liabilities rich · equity arb thinPrefer seasoned mezzanine over primary equity estimate
1

Signal — what moved

sourced

September broke a three-month run of repricing dominance: new issue printed €7.6bn (17 deals) against €3.1bn of refi/reset, taking year-to-date issuance to €94.9bn · 229 deals. AAA new issue averaged 124 bps while refi/reset AAA cleared at 120 bps — the year's tightest — a gap of only ~4 bps. BofA still expects around ~€60bn of refi/reset for the full year.

2

Forensic mechanism

estimate

Equity is the residual of the asset spread minus the weighted cost of liabilities, fees and expected losses. With loan spreads near ~355 bps and repricing waves still cutting collateral coupons, much of each basis point saved on the AAA is clawed back on the asset side. That is why managers prefer resetting existing liabilities, and why new equity is increasingly captive (manager-sponsored equity funds) rather than third-party. BB pricing is dispersing by manager: tiering, not beta, is the trade.

FORMULAPractitioner approximation estimate

$$\text{Arb}_{\text{equity}} \approx s_{\text{assets}} - \sum_{i} w_i \, s_i - f_{\text{mgmt}} - \ell_{\text{exp}}$$

s = spread, w = tranche weight, f = management fees, ℓ = expected credit loss. A practitioner's excess-spread approximation, not a deal model: it ignores reinvestment, OC diversion and timing. When asset spreads fall faster than the weighted liability cost, equity cash-on-cash compresses even at record-tight AAA levels. No liability weights or fee loads were captured this cycle, so it is not evaluated numerically.

Altice International — same sponsor, same playbook, new battlefield

Altice France is finished; the fight has moved to Altice International, where asset designations and a default notice have put recovery into court.

Restated fromE03E04
Adversarial · litigation-ledHard veto — watch list only estimate
1

Signal — what moved

sourced

Altice France closed its restructuring: debt cut by roughly ~€8.5bn, leverage to about ~4.6x, creditors taking about ~45% of the equity. The contest has moved to Altice International. In November 2025 Altice Portugal and Altice Caribbean were designated unrestricted and new money of €750m at 12% (2029) was raised against them. The 2028 secured group served a default notice on 14 Jul 2026; an action is now before the New York Supreme Court. Roughly ~€8bn of secured debt is in play, about ~€3.1bn matures in 2027, and the bonds trade in the mid-to-low 60s.

2

Forensic mechanism

estimate

The playbook is value migration: move crown-jewel assets outside the restricted group by unrestricted-subsidiary designation, raise priming new money against them, then negotiate from strength with the creditors left behind. Recovery for the legacy secured group becomes a function of the collateral left in the box, the court's reading of the indenture, and how hard the maturity wall presses the sponsor.

FORMULAStylised framework — not calibrated illustrative

$$\mathbb{E}[R] = P_{\text{win}} \, R_{\text{restore}} + \left(1 - P_{\text{win}}\right) R_{\text{residual}} - c_{\text{lit}}$$

A decision-analysis framing, not a market pricing formula: P_win is the probability the court unwinds or penalises the designations; c_lit is litigation and time cost. None of the inputs is observable today and no price is captured (gap G4), so it structures the question rather than answering it.

Signal — what moved

sourced

The Class B Transaction Support Agreement is backed by holders of >60% of the roughly ~£1bn outstanding: a full write-off in exchange for about ~5.9% recovered via equity, plus rights to join a £150m equity raise. Liquidity is the binding constraint: £2.65bn of £3bn of the emergency facility is drawn, cash runs out around late 2026 to early 2027, and the company is asking for a further £2.4bn to last into autumn 2027 — a request that worries super-senior holders about dilution. 2

Forensic mechanism

estimate

Whole-business securitisation protects senior creditors through ring-fencing and step-in rights, but the regulator's tolerance sets the ceiling. A recapitalisation needs Ofwat concessions on penalties, the investment programme and returns; without them the Special Administration Regime becomes the political default. SAR protects service continuity, not creditor value, and freezes timelines.

FORMULAIdentity — exact by construction derived

$$R_{\text{junior}} = \frac{\max\left(0,\; V_{\text{EV}} - D_{\text{super-senior}} - D_{\text{Class A}}\right)}{D_{\text{Class B}}}$$

Junior recovery is the residual after super-senior and Class A claims under strict absolute priority. Real outcomes are negotiated: the Class B TSA grants an equity recovery even though the residual may be zero, which is exactly the deviation this identity exposes. No enterprise value was captured this cycle.

T4 · TOPIC 4 OF 6

The default cycle is turning — quietly, through the back door

Defaults are rising faster than forecasters expected, and the channel is repeat defaults and LMEs rather than headline bankruptcies.

Restated fromE06E08
Rising · under-priced in liabilitiesRaise default assumptions; respect the OC maths estimate
1

Signal — what moved

sourced

Fitch's European leveraged loan trailing default rate reached 3.6% in September, up from 2.7% in August and 2.0% at mid-year. In July Fitch raised its full-year forecast to 4.0–4.5% from 3.0–3.5%. The Market Concern list — loans Fitch sees at risk of default — stands at 10.3–10.6%, the highest since early 2013. Repeat defaults and liability-management exercises are a material driver across an index of >€300bn.

2

Forensic mechanism

estimate

LMEs count as defaults when they impose losses, but they often leave the borrower alive and inside CLO portfolios at lower ratings. The damage arrives through CCC buckets and par erosion rather than through a wave of liquidations. When CCC holdings exceed the permitted bucket, the excess is carried at market value in OC tests; failing a junior OC test diverts cash from equity and mezzanine to deleveraging.

FORMULAMarket-standard definition, simplified derived

$$\text{OC}_j = \frac{\text{Par}_{\text{perf}} + \text{MV}_{\text{def}} - H_{\text{CCC excess}}}{\sum_{i \le j} \text{Par}_i}$$

The market-standard shape of a CLO overcollateralisation test, simplified: each indenture defines its own haircuts, carrying values and trigger levels. Performing par plus defaulted assets at market value, less the haircut on CCC holdings above the bucket, over the par of tranche j and everything senior to it. Junior OC is where a rising default rate bites first — trustee data is the missing input (gap G1).

3

  • LME prevalence in sponsor-backed capital structures.
  • Software and AI-exposed sectors facing revenue-model pressure.
  • Refinancing wall for weaker credits over the next two years.
  • Speed of rating migration into CCC.

Private credit — 'bad PIK' is the shadow default rate

PIK switched on after origination signals cash-flow stress that headline default rates miss — and there is still no reliable public series to measure it.

Restated fromE09
Opaque · stress in 2021–22 vintagesDemand PIK disclosure before any allocation estimate
1

Signal — what moved

sourced

Advisers and regulators distinguish good PIK, structured at origination, from bad PIK, switched on later to preserve liquidity. Commentary concentrates stress in 2021–22 vintages and in software and SaaS borrowers exposed to AI disruption. No quantified public series of bad-PIK incidence was found this cycle (gap G3).

2

Forensic mechanism

estimate

A coupon converted to PIK preserves the borrower's cash but grows the claim: loan-to-value drifts up while the loan stays 'performing'. Marks lag, so NAV-based reporting understates stress until a realisation event — refinancing, sale or default — forces price discovery.

FORMULAIdentity — exact by construction derived

$$D_t = D_0 \left(1 + r_{\text{PIK}}\right)^{t}, \qquad \text{LTV}_t = \frac{D_t}{V_t}$$

Compounding arithmetic, exact by construction: under PIK the claim grows while enterprise value may stagnate, so LTV rises without a single missed payment. No PIK rates or valuations were captured (gap G3).

3

Factor map

estimate
  • Share of portfolio income received in kind versus in cash.
  • Amendment frequency and covenant resets.
  • Vintage concentration at 2021–22 entry valuations.
  • Sector exposure to AI-driven disruption (software and SaaS).
  • Valuation governance: who marks, how often, on what evidence.

The FPC keeps private credit and leveraged lending on its vulnerability list, and now links AI capex financing to the same channels.

Restated fromE07
Systemic watch · no trigger yetTrack regulation; hold convexity estimate
1

Signal — what moved

sourced

The Financial Policy Committee continues to flag private credit and leveraged lending as vulnerabilities: opacity, layered leverage, and a maturity wall managed through amend-and-extend. It highlights that AI-related capex is increasingly financed through private credit, leveraged finance and structured finance. Its system-wide exploratory scenario work is mapping how non-bank stress transmits.

2

Forensic mechanism

estimate

Leverage multiplies through layers: a fund borrows against its NAV, the portfolio company is itself levered, and the lender may be financed by bank back-leverage. Each layer looks modest; the product does not. Shocks transmit through margin calls and redemptions rather than direct defaults.

FORMULAStylised framework — not calibrated illustrative

$$L_{\text{eff}} = \prod_{k=1}^{n} L_k$$

A stylised illustration of how exposure compounds across layers (fund NAV facility × portfolio-company leverage × lender back-leverage). It is not a Bank of England metric and is not calibrated: no layer multiples were captured this cycle.
