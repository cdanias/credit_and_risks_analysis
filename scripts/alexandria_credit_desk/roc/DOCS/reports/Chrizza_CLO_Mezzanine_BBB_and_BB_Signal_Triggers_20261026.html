1. The "Barbell Squeeze" — The Extremes of the Stack Are Broken

Look at what is happening at the two poles of the CLO capital structure today:

  │      AAA / AA           │  ◄── CROWDED & PRICED FOR PERFECTION (120–124 bps)
  │    (Senior Debt)        │      Negative convexity: zero credit spread, all mark-to-market risk.
  ├─────────────────────────┤
  │      A / BBB            │  ◄── 🎯 THE INVESTMENT GRADE MEZZANINE SWEET SPOT

  │        BB               │  ◄── 🎯 THE HIGH-YIELD MEZZANINE ASYMMETRY

  ├─────────────────────────┤

  • The Top (AAA/AA) is an Indexer’s Trap: Japanese agricultural banks (Norinchukin), US insurers under Solvency II / RBC rules, and the explosion of CLO ETFs (like JAAA) have created an insatiable, mechanical bid. They have dragged European AAA spreads to 120 bps (refi/reset) and 124 bps (new issue)—the tights of the year. The Risk: You take pure duration and spread-widening risk for a meager 120 bps over Euribor. If macro volatility spikes or loan defaults accelerate, AAAs widen to 160–180 bps, handing you severe mark-to-market drawdowns with zero credit cushion. You are picking up pennies in front of a steamroller.
  • The Bottom (Equity & Single-B) is an Arbitrage Illusion: Collateral loan coupons have been aggressively compressed by sponsor repricing waves down to ~355 bps. Meanwhile, the default rate is climbing (3.6% TTM, forecast 4.0%–4.5% by Fitch), and the Market Concern list is at 10.6% (a 13-year high). The Trap: When loans get downgraded into CCC or slip below 80 cents, they breach the deal's 7.5% CCC bucket limit. The excess par is marked at market value, eroding the Junior Overcollateralization (OC) cushion. If that OC test trips by even 10 basis points, the waterfall enforces a mandatory Interest Diversion: all cash flows to equity are immediately zeroed and diverted to amortize senior notes. In equity, you are taking unhedged corporate distress risk for an arbitrage spread that is already vanishing.

2. The Mathematical Reality of Loss Absorption (The "Cushion Math")

Why can a BB or BBB tranche survive what terrifies the equity holder? Look at the hard indenture arithmetic:

  • In a standard European CLO 2.0 structure, a BB tranche sits on top of ~10.0% to 14.0% of subordination (the 8% equity tranche + 2% single-B tranche + accumulated par surplus).
  • A BBB tranche sits on top of ~15.0% to 20.0% of subordination.

Now, run the default stress test:

$$\text{Cumulative Collateral Loss} = \text{Cumulative Defaults} \times (1 - \text{Recovery Rate})$$

Assume the European loan default rate surges to 5.0% annually for three consecutive years (a brutal, protracted recession scenario), totaling 15.0% cumulative defaults. Even if historical recoveries fall from their 65% norm down to a depressed 50%, the cumulative net credit loss to the collateral pool is:

$$15.0\% \times (1 - 0.50) = 7.50\% \text{ Net Par Loss}$$

What happens to the stack?

  • The 7.50% net loss is entirely absorbed by the Equity tranche (8.0%).
  • The Single-B tranche (2.0%) takes a minor nick or remains whole.
  • The BB tranche (sitting at 10%–14% subordination) suffers ZERO principal impairment.
  • The BBB tranche (sitting at 15%–20% subordination) remains completely untouched.

Yet, the market prices BB tranches at Euribor + 600 to 750 basis points (delivering an unlevered floating-rate yield of ~9.5% to 11.0%), and BBBs at Euribor + 350 to 450 bps (~7.0% to 8.0%). You are getting paid high-yield equity-like yields for an instrument protected by an iron wall of contractual junior absorption!

3. The Counter-Intuitive "Deleveraging Windfall"

Here is the secret of the indenture waterfall that only seasoned restructuring specialists appreciate:

When the junior credit environment deteriorates and the Overcollateralization (OC) test trips, it is BAD for Equity, but it is a MASSIVE STRUCTURAL WINDFALL for BBB and BB Mezzanine.

Why? Because under the deal indenture, when the Junior OC test trips, all cash flow that would have been paid to equity is sequestered and used to prepay the AAA notes at par.

As senior AAA notes are paid down:

  1. The total debt outstanding shrinks.
  2. The percentage of subordination protecting the remaining BBB and BB tranches increases automatically (a structural phenomenon known as subordination growth or deleveraging).
  3. The mezzanine tranche migrates from being a subordinate note to becoming, effectively, the senior-most debt in a de-risked structure.

The very crisis that bankrupts the equity holder actively fortifies the mezzanine holder!

4. The Signal Trigger: Manager Dispersion Has Exploded

At the AAA level, there is almost no dispersion. A tier-one manager (e.g. Blackstone, CVC, Carlyle, Barings) prints AAAs at 122 bps; a tier-three, boutique manager prints at 126 bps. The spread differential is a trivial 4 basis points.

At the BB level, dispersion has blown out to over 200 basis points:

  • Top-tier managers’ BB tranches trade around Euribor + 580–620 bps.
  • Weaker, tier-three managers’ BB tranches trade at Euribor + 750–850+ bps.

The Trigger: The market is finally discriminating between managers who know how to trade credit and managers who merely warehouse beta.

  • Weak managers hold dirty portfolios riddled with B3-negative credits, aggressive sponsor carve-outs, and restructuring liabilities (e.g. Altice, Thames Water, Ardagh).
  • Elite managers have active trading desks that ruthlessly purge deteriorating loans at 88 cents before they crash to 60 cents and hit the CCC trap.

In BBBs and BBs, we can buy the tier-one manager at the tier-two spread. That is where alpha lives.

5. Why "Seasoned Secondary" Over Primary?

In primary issuance, you are buying a blind pool. The manager has 6 to 12 months to ramp the warehouse. They are forced to buy whatever syndicated loans are clearing the market, often accepting covenant-lite paper and thin spreads (~355 bps) just to fill the vehicle.

In seasoned secondary paper (deals issued in 2022–2024 that have 1 to 3 years of seasoned track record):

  1. Zero Blind Pool Risk: Every single loan inside the portfolio is known, marked, and audited. We can run our covenant parser across every single borrower credit agreement.
  2. Built-In Par Surplus: Seasoned deals have already bought back loans at secondary discounts during prior volatility spikes, building a cushion of par surplus inside the vehicle.
  3. Closing Reinvestment Windows: As these deals approach the end of their reinvestment period, the manager is prohibited from reinvesting prepayments into low-yielding new loans; instead, cash must be returned to amortize the notes, rapidly de-risking our principal.
